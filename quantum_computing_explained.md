## The Core: What's a Qubit?

Your laptop runs on bits. Each bit is either a 0 or a 1. Everything—every photo, every spreadsheet, every AI generation—comes down to billions of zeros and ones being flipped.

Quantum computers use **qubits** (quantum bits). Unlike regular bits, qubits can be both 0 and 1 *at the same time*. This is called **superposition**.

Think of a coin. A classical bit is the coin when it's heads *or* tails—decided. A qubit is the coin spinning in the air—technically both until you look.

Now here's the mind-bender: 50 qubits can represent 1 quadrillion states *simultaneously*. That's not a typo. A quantum computer doesn't process possibilities one by one like your laptop. It processes them all at once.

## The Magic: Three Quantum Effects

Quantum computing isn't just about superposition. Three effects make it powerful:

### 1. Superposition
Qubits can be in multiple states at once, exploring many solutions simultaneously.

### 2. Entanglement
When qubits become entangled, measuring one *instantly* affects the other—regardless of distance. Einstein called this "spooky action at a distance." It's real, and it's the key to quantum computing's power.

### 3. Interference
Quantum computers amplify correct answers and cancel wrong ones—think of it as fine-tuning the results.

## The Problem: Decoherence

Here's why we don't have quantum laptops yet.

Qubits are incredibly fragile. Vibration. Temperature changes. Even cosmic rays. Any interference collapses the quantum state (decoherence).

Current quantum computers operate at near absolute zero—about -273°C. That's colder than outer space. The slightest noise destroys the calculation.

That's why error correction is so important. Google's 2026 breakthrough achieved "below threshold" error correction—meaning quantum computers can now be reliable enough for practical use.

## How a Quantum Computer Actually Works

Here's the process:

1. **Initialize** — Set qubits to starting state (|0⟩)
2. **Encode** — Transform your problem into quantum states
3. **Compute** — Apply quantum gates (like Hadamard for superposition, CNOT for entanglement)
4. **Measure** — The superposition collapses to a binary result

It's like running a calculation in parallel universes and then asking which one holds the answer.
